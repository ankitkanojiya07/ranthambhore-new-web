
# **2b. Ranthambore National Park — Overview & Geography**

***Meta Title: Ranthambore National Park Overview | Area, Location, Landscape & Core Zones***

***Meta Description: Everything you need to know about Ranthambore National Park — its location, size, geography, lakes, and the 10 safari zones that make up this magnificent tiger reserve.***

## **Location & Access**

Ranthambore National Park is located in the Sawai Madhopur district of southeastern Rajasthan, India. Sawai Madhopur town is the nearest railhead — just 14 km from the park's main gate — and is well connected to Delhi (about 5–6 hours by train), Jaipur (3 hours), and Agra (3 hours). The park sits at an elevation ranging from 215 to 505 metres above sea level.

## **Area & Boundaries**

The core national park covers approximately 392 sq km. When the buffer zones are included, the total Ranthambore Tiger Reserve spans around 1,334 sq km. The park is bounded by the Banas River to the north and the Chambal River to the south, with the Aravalli hills to the west and Vindhya ranges to the east.

## **Landscape & Water Bodies**

The park's topography is diverse — rocky outcrops, deep gorges, open grasslands, and dense forests create a rich mosaic of habitats. Three major lakes — Padam Talab, Raj Bagh Talab, and Malik Talab — are the heart of the park's ecosystem. These lakes attract extraordinary wildlife activity, especially in the dry summer months when animals congregate around water. The ruins of ancient chhatris, mosques, and walls scattered across the landscape add a haunting, romantic quality unlike any other national park in India.

## **Vegetation**

The dominant vegetation type is tropical dry deciduous forest, with dhok (Anogeissus pendula) being the most common tree, covering roughly 80% of the forested area. Banyan, pipal, flame of the forest (palash), mango, and jamun trees are also common. The undergrowth includes grasses, shrubs, and creepers that provide cover for smaller mammals and ground-nesting birds.
